What kids do

A typical day, from COOKSMART's camp page (checked Nov 21, 2026).

9:00
Arrive, wash hands, put on aprons, set up stations, and a food-identification challenge.
10:00
Cook the day's hot lunch: safe chopping (claw and bridge grips), measuring, and heat safety.
12:00
Eat the lunch they cooked, then indoor games and social time. Half day goes home at 1:30.
1:30–4:00
Full day only: holiday baking, afternoon snacks, food science experiments, sewing, mindfulness and holiday crafts.

Half-day campers also learn life skills, make a fruit snack and do some baking (COOKSMART's half-day booking page). The camp page doesn't say at what time.

What they learn

Baking
Measuring spices, rolling dough, cookie cutters and icing. Treats come home to share.
Crafts
Seasonal crafts, including cinnamon salt-dough ornaments.
Life skills
Beginner sewing, money basics, table manners, and cleaning up their station.
Food science
Experiments with yeast and baking soda, taste tests, and how food fuels a growing body.

From COOKSMART's camp page, checked Nov 21, 2026. The page doesn't say which of these half-day campers do; sewing, crafts and food science are listed for full-day afternoons.

Parent questions

Tap a question for the answer. "Ask the camp" means COOKSMART's pages don't say.

Is there before or after care?Ask the camp
No before or after care is listed for winter camp. (COOKSMART's summer camp page offers an extra afternoon session to 6:00, but the winter page doesn't.) Ask the camp before booking.
What about lunch and special diets?Lunch and snacks included
Both half and full day include lunch and healthy snacks that the kids make themselves. Half day includes a fruit snack and baking; full day adds afternoon snacks. COOKSMART says recipes are vegetarian and halal-friendly; gluten-free and dairy needs can be handled with advance notice.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · camp website and booking pages
Allergy and nut policy?Nut-free
COOKSMART says the facility and all ingredients are 100% peanut- and tree-nut-free. Families give full allergy and diet details at registration. For a life-threatening allergy, your child must carry their own unexpired EpiPen (a waist pouch is suggested) and you fill in a Medical Confirmation form. Staff are trained to use an EpiPen.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · camp website, camps page and parent info
Can my child take medication at camp?Yes, with a form
You fill in a Medical Confirmation form. Medicine comes in its original pharmacy-labelled container, one day's dose at a time, with any measuring spoon labelled. Kids take it themselves under staff supervision; if they can't, you need to arrange someone else to give it.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · COOKSMART parent info for camps
What if my child gets sick?You'll be called
Keep sick children at home. If a child gets sick at camp, COOKSMART calls you to pick them up.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · COOKSMART parent info for camps
What's the staff ratio?Ask the camp
No number is given. COOKSMART says class sizes are "strictly limited" with small-group coaching.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · camp website
Are staff checked and trained?Yes
Instructors are police-checked (vulnerable sector), certified food handlers, and trained in first aid and CPR. Camp staff are also certified in allergy awareness, and most have HIGH FIVE or university child-development training.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · camp website and parent info
Do kids use real knives and ovens?Child-safe knives
Kids use child-safe nylon and serrated knives on non-slip prep mats, with small-group coaching. Only instructors handle hot ovens.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · camp website
How big are the groups?Ask the camp
Kids are grouped into age-appropriate teams: older kids do more advanced chopping and kitchen math, younger ones build the basics. Class sizes are "strictly limited"; numbers not given.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · camp website
Can siblings or friends be together?Where possible
COOKSMART says it groups siblings or friends together where possible. Tell them when you register.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · COOKSMART FAQ
Are there discounts?Sibling code, membership
Siblings: book quantity 2 with code CSFAMILY2; the winter page doesn't say how much it saves (on COOKSMART's teen page the same code takes $25 off each extra teen). COOKSMART Club: a yearly family membership gives 20% off all camps, winter included. Its page says $149 a year, but the checkout box on the same page says CA$169: ask which is right. Gift certificates of $25 and $50 can be used toward any program.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · booking pages, membership page
Phones, toys and devices?Leave them home
COOKSMART asks campers to leave toys and personal items at home. Any device brought to camp stays in a bag, not a pocket.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · COOKSMART parent info for camps
Does my child need to be toilet trained?Ask the camp
COOKSMART's pages don't say. Ask before booking, especially for 5-year-olds.
Special needs support?Ask the camp
COOKSMART runs autism-friendly, sensory-conscious programs for schools and lists Autism Ontario and Shining Stars as partners, but its camp pages don't describe support at camp. Ask before booking.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · inclusive programs page
Do they go outside in winter?Ask the camp
COOKSMART's general parent info says campers go outside every day and should dress for the weather. The winter camp page only mentions indoor games. Ask which applies, and pack warm clothes to be safe.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · COOKSMART parent info for camps
Swimming or field trips?None listed
Neither appears in the winter camp's activities.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · camp website
What should we pack?Water, shoes, apron
A reusable water bottle, indoor shoes and an apron (camp aprons are available too). Hair tied back, sleeves that roll up. Label everything. Equipment and ingredients are provided.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · camp website and parent info
Cancellation and refunds?Tiered
In writing, separately for each child. More than a month before: refund minus $60. 2–4 weeks: minus $100. 2 weeks or less: minus 50%. Within 72 hours, or once camp starts: no refund. COOKSMART's FAQ adds that credits toward future programs may be possible. How these tiers apply to single days isn't stated: ask the camp.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · cancellation policy and FAQ
Subsidy or tax receipt?Ask the camp
No subsidy or tax receipt information is listed. COOKSMART's FAQ says it occasionally offers promotions; contact them directly.
Licensed or accredited?Insured, no accreditation listed
COOKSMART says it is insured and covered by WSIB. No camp accreditation is listed.Verified Nov 21, 2026 · about page

What parents say

5.0 on Google from 50 reviews (Nov 21, 2026). We read all 50: 48 five-star, 2 four-star, no complaints. About 13 have no written comment.

  • Kids come home excited, describe what they made, and start cooking at home.
  • Staff are described as warm, patient and organized, with parents greeted at the door and signed in and out each day.
  • Parents like not packing lunches or snacks.
  • Groups can mix ages. One parent noted a large group with a wide age range that still stayed engaged; another said an older child who usually avoids camps for younger kids enjoyed it.
  • Staff respected families' dietary preferences.

Most reviews are about summer camps, classes, online classes and parties. None we found are specifically about winter camp. COOKSMART is also on the 2026 Consumer Choice Award list for Peel Region (Cooking Classes).
